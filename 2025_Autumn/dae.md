As India celebrates the Platinum Jubilee (1954–2024) of the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE), the nation takes pride in 70 years of groundbreaking achievements in atomic science and technology. Established in 1954, DAE has remained at the forefront of India's strategic, scientific, and societal progress through peaceful applications of nuclear energy.

This landmark year not only commemorates past accomplishments but also signals India’s readiness to embrace future opportunities and challenges in nuclear science and technology with continued dedication to innovation, scientific excellence, and public welfare.

- - -

### DAE at 70: Powering a Nation, Enabling Progress

Over the last seven decades, DAE has been instrumental in developing indigenous nuclear capabilities most notably, by establishing numerous nuclear reactors across India that have significantly contributed to the country’s energy grid. These reactors not only enhance energy security but also help in reducing the carbon footprint, aligning India with its global climate commitments.

DAE’s leadership extends beyond energy. It has revolutionized fields like healthcare, agriculture, and industrial processing through the applications of radiation technology. From developing cancer treatment and diagnostic tools to improving crop yield and soil fertility, DAE’s innovations have reached every corner of society.

At the global stage, DAE has positioned India as a responsible atomic power, advocating for strict safety standards and international cooperation in nuclear governance.

- - -

### The Backbone of India’s Atomic Infrastructure: Key DAE Units

DAE functions through several core units, each playing a pivotal role in its mission. Some of the prominent ones include:

1. Uranium Corporation of India Limited (UCIL)
2. Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC)
3. Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR)
4. Atomic Energy Regulatory Board (AERB)
5. Nuclear Power Corporation of India Limited (NPCIL)
6. Atomic Minerals Directorate for Exploration and Research (AMD)
7. Electronics Corporation of India Limited (ECIL)
8. Heavy Water Board (HWB)
9. Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR)
10. Homi Bhabha National Institute (HBNI)

These organizations collectively represent the spine of India’s nuclear sector, contributing to power generation, fundamental research, industrial applications, defence, and socio-economic development.

- - -

### Spotlight on UCIL Tummalapalle: A Symbol of Innovation and Sustainability

One of the shining examples of DAE’s success story is the Uranium Corporation of India Limited (UCIL), particularly its Tummalapalle Unit in Kadapa District, Andhra Pradesh which is only 221 kms away from IIT Tirupati. Established in 2007, this site has grown into a world-class facility recognized for advanced uranium mining and processing.

Tummalapalle sits atop one of the world’s largest uranium reserves. The unit utilizes cutting-edge underground mining techniques and eco-conscious processing technologies, ensuring efficient production with minimal environmental impact. This supports India's goal of energy independence while reducing reliance on fossil fuels.

Beyond its operational success, the Tummalapalle unit is also committed to community development. Through investments in local education, healthcare, infrastructure, and livelihood generation, UCIL is helping transform surrounding communities while upholding safety and sustainability.

### Marking the Jubilee: Celebrating the Past, Inspiring the Future

To commemorate DAE's 70-year milestone, UCIL Tummalapalle has planned a series of celebratory events, including:
- Guest lectures by eminent scientists and alumni
- Educational tours for local school and college students
- Inauguration of new initiatives
- Sports and cultural programs
- Public outreach activities showcasing the unit’s achievements

These events aim to educate, engage, and inspire the next generation of scientists and citizens about the importance of atomic energy and responsible resource management.

### Conclusion: A Future Charged with Promise

The Platinum Jubilee of the Department of Atomic Energy is not merely a celebration of the past—it is a reaffirmation of India’s commitment to using atomic energy for peaceful, progressive, and sustainable development. As facilities like UCIL Tummalapalle continue to play a pivotal role, the legacy of DAE will remain a cornerstone in India’s journey towards scientific excellence and energy sovereignty.
