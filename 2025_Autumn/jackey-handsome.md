Jack Anderson was a computer science engineer at a mid-sized but fast-growing tech firm in Chicago. He lived alone in a modest apartment just close enough to downtown to commute quickly but far enough to avoid the chaos. His alarm clock went off at exactly 6:30 every morning. He would snooze once, then drag himself into the shower, make toast, brew a strong black coffee, and head out in the same blue shirt and pants he’d ironed the night before. Jack wasn’t lazy; he was efficient. Or at least that’s what he told himself.

Work was repetitive. He maintained old systems and sometimes added minor features for clients who barely used them. His colleagues were friendly enough, but Jack was always quiet, almost to the point of being invisible. He had lunch every day at the same corner restaurant: a sandwich, chips, and Coke. He’d scroll through tech blogs during lunch, more out of habit now than interest. Life moved in loops.

Jack had a crush on his coworker, Emily Thompson. She was vibrant and full of life, always laughing with others, always the centre of attention during happy hours or team meetings. Jack adored her from afar. He had worked up the courage once to ask her out during a Friday team lunch, and she had smiled awkwardly and said, “You’re sweet, Jack, but I just don’t think we’d vibe. You’re just... very formal."

He went home early that day and didn’t return to work the next day.

To top it off, his long-awaited promotion was denied. He overheard his manager telling someone else, “Jack’s a great guy, dependable, but he just doesn’t seem to grow. He’s the same guy he was last year." That comment stuck with Jack like a shadow.

Every two weeks, Jack trimmed his beard in front of his slightly cracked bathroom mirror. It wasn’t much-just some stubble removal and an occasional touch-up to maintain his bland corporate look. But this time, something inside him rebelled. Maybe it was the repetition of the same cycle every day. He was crying inside for a change. So, Jack decided to change.

He stared at himself for a long time, razor in hand, then methodically shaped his facial hair into a circle beard. It wasn’t anything too wild, but it added a sense of shape and character to his face. It made his features pop. He looked... different. Edgier. Maybe even bold.

Regardless of the initial excitement, he left home that day feeling the same as any other day. But the office wasn’t the same.

As he walked through the doors, conversations stopped. People glanced at him, then did a double-take. Some whispered. Some smiled. Some even looked intimidated. He had to double-check his zipper and look at his shoes-was there something wrong? But no. It was his beard.

“Jack, wow," said Sarah from marketing, pausing by the coffee machine. “That’s a new look. Looks good on you."

Jack blinked. “Thanks. Just felt like changing things up."

By 10 a.m., the entire floor had seen him. Some of the guys in his department-especially the newer hires-looked at him differently. Not mockingly, but with cautious curiosity. One guy, Mike, who barely spoke to Jack before, now asked if they could grab lunch together sometime. Women smiled at him with something more than politeness.

He noticed Brian, the tall, gym-hardened guy from sales-also the same guy Jack knew Emily used to swoon over-lingering nearby. Brian approached with a smug grin and said, “New beard, huh? Trying something cool?"

“Just felt like it," Jack replied.

Brian scoffed lightly. “You looked sharper clean-shaven. Just saying." But there was a flicker of something-jealousy? Insecurity? Jack wasn’t sure.

At noon, Emily came to his desk.

“Jack, wow, I didn’t think you’d actually take my advice."

“What advice?" Jack asked confused.

“About growing a beard. I said that at our New Year’s party, remember?"

Jack didn’t remember. He hadn’t even attended that party. But he smiled. “Yeah. Glad I did."

Emily’s voice softened. “It suits you. Want to grab dinner tonight?"

Jack almost laughed in disbelief. “Sure."

Just after 3 p.m., his manager, Mr. Reynolds, called him in.

“Jack, I’m promoting you to team leader."

Jack’s jaw dropped. “What? I... I don’t know what to say."

“You’ve been solid, but I wanted to see some spark. Leadership. Initiative. I see it now."

Mr. Reynolds snapped a selfie with Jack to commemorate the moment and posted it on LinkedIn. It went viral. Jack didn’t even have social media, but by 5 p.m., everyone in the office was talking about “the guy who got promoted for being cool and competent."

The next day, Jack’s beard was trending. Hashtags like #JackeyHandsome, #BeardConfidence, and #TechChic took over Tweet and Instargram. Beard grooming companies started capitalizing on the trend, releasing special “Jack Style" beard kits. Celebrities grew out their facial hair overnight. Talk shows speculated about Jack’s identity. Articles dubbed him “The Influencer."

He started getting invited to tech conferences, beard care product launches, even film festivals. A small studio quickly produced a movie titled ‘Jackey Handsome’ starring a popular young actor. It became a surprise hit.

At work, everything changed. People sought Jack’s opinion on everything from code issues to office decor. Junior developers treated him like a master. Senior staff sought his blessing before rolling out new ideas. He was scared if the attention. When asked things he didn’t know, he referred them to colleagues-earning even more accidental respect that he was being humble.

Jack started dating Emily. She bragged about him online, posted pictures, even got free meals by tagging brands in couple selfies. Jack was overwhelmed but went along. After all, things were finally looking up.

While on a second date with Emily, a bulky man approached their table at a sushi bar and glared at Jack.

“You’re a symbol of patriarchal conformity!" he barked. That beard sends a message!" he barked.

Before Jack could react, Emily pulled him away. They fled the restaurant, but their Uber driver recognized Jack and insisted on taking them to his home to introduce him to his children. “You’re a hero to young boys," he said.

Feeling creeped out, they left in another car. As they neared Emily’s apartment, a young woman jumped in front of them, screaming, “He belongs with me, not you!" She tried to attack Emily. A police officer restrained her, but not before slipping Jack his business card. “In case you ever need anything," he said, holding Jack’s hand a second too long.

Jack was speechless.

By now, he was scared to go out. Paparazzi waited outside his building. Fans mailed gifts. His photo was painted onto murals. Brands used his face without permission. He never approved any of it.
Two weeks passed.

It was beard trim day.

He looked at himself in the mirror, comparing his current bearded look to an old selfie from his phone. The clean-shaven version of himself looked honest, peaceful, familiar and simply better. The bearded version felt... not him. May be for men in general, beard looks good, but for him, clean shave looks better.

He shaved.

The next morning, when he walked into the office, silence fell again-but this time, it was cold.
Whispers turned into stares. Some people laughed under their breath. Brian walked by and muttered, “Gave up?" Emily didn’t even say hello. Her Instargram now featured heavily-filtered selfies with captions like “Some men just can’t handle being men."

At 2 p.m., Mr. Reynolds called him in.

“We’re reversing the promotion. You’re not who we thought you were."

“What changed?"

“You can leave now."

Later that day, beard stock prices dropped dramatically. The movie ‘Jackey Handsome’ was pulled from several theaters after protestors called it “toxic and misleading." News channels covered “The Fall of a men’s makeup products" People burned fake beards in symbolic protests.

Jack's inbox flooded with hate mail. Memes labeled him “Jackey Unhandsome" and “Beardless Betrayer."

Emily broke up with him in a single text: “This won’t work"

Jack sat alone in the break room, eyes on his coffee. No one joined him.

That night, walking home, he stared at the dark sky. He felt used, discarded, like the world had chosen to play with his identity and toss it away. But he smiled to himself in confusion and the stupidity of it. He thought how silly this world was for getting so stirred up on the smallest things. He craved something real. Not trends. Not social media fame. Just something-someone-genuine.

At a road crossing, he waited silently. Across the street, a girl in a hoodie smiled at him. She waved. Jack felt his heart skip. He waved back. She blushed. She made a gesture to ask him if he is free this evening and he replied yes shyly.

For the first time in weeks, he smiled.

He got on his bus.

The girl zipped down her hoodie, revealing a T-shirt with the same band logo as Jack’s. She thought he was a fellow fan of “Cloud Parade"-her favorite indie band. Jack’s shirt, however, had come from a discount bin. His dad had bought it for $5 because it “looked like something young people wear."

The girl watched Jack leave. She smiled to herself. “He loves them too," she whispered.

Jack leaned against the window, watching the city blur past. Maybe real connection was still possible. Maybe, just maybe, life wasn’t meant to be a performance. Maybe all he needed was to be Jack.
